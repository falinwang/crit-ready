# Chapter 10: Dealing with Changes

## Core Idea
Stakeholder changes are inevitable and represent an opportunity to build trust, manage expectations, and balance compromises.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Bank Account of Trust**:
  - When to use: Throughout stakeholder relationships to gauge how much capital you have.
  - How: Make deposits via good work and agreement; withdrawals happen when you disagree or fail. Maintain a high balance so stakeholders trust you on difficult design decisions.

## Key Concepts
- **Compromise**: Relenting on a design decision to make a deposit in the trust bank account.
- **The Blame Game**: Stakeholders looking for a scapegoat; overcome it by owning mistakes quickly.

## Mental Models
Think of relationship capital as a bank account. You cannot make a withdrawal (disagreeing with a stakeholder) if you have not made enough deposits (agreeing, delivering on time, active listening).

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring mistakes**: Denying responsibility breaks trust. Instead, own up to mistakes quickly to build more trust.
- **Obsessing over history**: Over-explaining why a mistake happened sounds like an excuse. Focus on the fix instead.

## Key Takeaways
1. Sometimes you have to relent to make a deposit in the trust bank account.
2. Admitting you're wrong builds trust faster than hiding it.
3. Be direct about mistakes, then immediately pivot to the solution.
4. Pick your battles based on your current trust balance.
