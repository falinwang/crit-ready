# Chapter 11: How Executives Can Help Designers

## Core Idea
Executives and designers must bridge the gap between business strategy and design execution through mutual understanding and clear communication.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The King and the Blind Man**:
  - When to use: When communicating across the power and context divide.
  - How: The executive (King) has power but lacks ground-level context. The designer (Blind Man) has ground-level context but lacks power. Both must share their unique perspective to succeed.

## Key Concepts
- **A Seat at the Table**: Designers participating in strategic business decisions, not just execution.
- **Design Project Checklist**: A tool for executives to provide clear constraints and goals to designers.

## Mental Models
Think of executives not as adversaries, but as partners who possess the business context you lack, while you possess the user context they lack. 

## Anti-patterns
- **Dictating solutions**: Executives telling designers *how* to design something rather than *what* problem to solve.
- **Designing in a vacuum**: Designers ignoring business constraints and executive vision.

## Key Takeaways
1. Executives should focus on problems and goals, not pixel-level solutions.
2. Designers must proactively seek out the business context executives hold.
3. Use a checklist to align on expectations before design work begins.
4. Building executive trust earns design a strategic seat at the table.
