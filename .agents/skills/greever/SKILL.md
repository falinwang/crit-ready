## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

- **The IDEAL Framework**
  - **Identify the problem**: State the problem you are solving before talking about the solution.
  - **Describe your solution**: Explain what you did and how it works.
  - **Empathize with the user**: Show how your solution helps the user achieve their goals.
  - **Appeal to the business**: Connect the design decision back to business goals and metrics.
  - **Lock in agreement**: Secure approval or clear next steps before leaving the conversation.
  - *When to use*: Use this 5-step framework to structure your response anytime a stakeholder questions a design decision.

- **The Bank Account of Trust**
  - *Concept*: Every positive interaction (delivering on time, good communication) deposits trust. Every mistake or missed deadline withdraws it.
  - *Why it matters*: You need a high trust balance so stakeholders will give you the benefit of the doubt on risky or subjective design decisions.

- **Listen to Understand, Not to Respond**
  - *Concept*: Pause and process what stakeholders are saying (uncovering the real underlying problem) rather than immediately defending the design.
  - *How*: Use implicit listening (body language, note-taking) and explicit listening (echoing back, asking clarifying questions).

- **Lead with a YES**
  - *Concept*: Always start your response with a positive affirmation ("Yes, we can do that, but...", or "Yes, that's a great point...").
  - *Why*: It diffuses defensiveness and positions you as a partner, even if you ultimately disagree with their proposed solution.
